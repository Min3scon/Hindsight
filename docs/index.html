What it does

A long memory for every sculpt

Sculpting is a stream of small decisions. Hindsight keeps each one, so a good idea from an hour ago is never more than a drag away.

Rewind, stroke by stroke

Every brush stroke in Sculpt Mode becomes one step in that object’s history, and each object can hold up to 10,000 of them. Blender’s built-in undo is a short list by default; Hindsight works alongside it and keeps a far deeper record of how the form grew.

The scrubber

A slider sits along the bottom of the 3D viewport. Drag it and the mesh follows you backwards or forwards through the steps, live. A step counter shows where you are, and Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z still step one at a time.

Morph History brush

Choose a moment in the history and mark it with Set Morph Target Here. Then paint with the Morph History brush: the areas you touch drift back toward the shape they had at that moment, and everything you leave alone stays as it is now. Recover yesterday’s nose without losing today’s jaw.

Movie Mode

Play the history back on the Timeline as a timelapse. Press play and watch the sculpt build itself from the first stroke to the last, ready to screen-capture or just to enjoy.

Per-object and global history

By default every object has its own timeline, so rewinding one never disturbs another. Prefer a single shared timeline for the whole scene? Switch on global history, and Preserve Modes controls how modes are treated as you step through it.

Join and unmerge

Join sculpted objects with Ctrl+J and Hindsight merges their histories. Scrub back past the join and the original objects come back, each with its own history intact.

Persistent history, with crash recovery

History does not have to vanish when you close Blender. Save it in a sidecar folder next to the .blend, embed it inside the .blend, or do both. If Blender ever crashes, Crash Recovery can bring your history back, either from the last saved state or from the interrupted session.

Features

Everything in the box

  • 10,000-step rewind

    Up to 10,000 steps per object, one for every stroke, all of them one drag away.

  • Viewport scrubber

    A slider along the bottom of the 3D viewport that jumps to any step, with a live step counter.

  • History Menu

    The dot at the left end of the bar opens quick actions for the history of the active object.

  • Morph History brush

    Paint chosen areas back toward the shape they had at a step you marked as the morph target.

  • Movie Mode

    Play the whole history back on the Timeline as a timelapse of your sculpt.

  • Per-object history

    Each object keeps its own timeline, so rewinding one leaves the others alone.

  • Global history + Preserve Modes

    Switch to one shared timeline when you want it, with an option for how modes are handled.

  • Join / Unmerge

    Scrub back past a Ctrl+J join and the original objects return.

  • Separate & duplicate aware

    Separating and duplicating understand history, and warn about linked duplicates.

  • Masks, Face Sets, visibility, colours

    More than shape: masks, Face Sets, hidden geometry and colours are part of the history too.

  • Edit Mode grouping

    Edit Mode work is grouped into history steps, so a session reads as a few clear moments.

  • Voxel Remesh & Trim snapshots

    Operations that change topology are stored as full snapshots, so you can rewind straight through them.

  • Branching with truncation warning

    Sculpt after scrubbing back and the future branch is replaced, with a warning before it happens.

  • Collection filtering

    Choose which collections are tracked, so history is only kept where you want it.

  • Sidecar / Embed / Both saving

    Keep history in a sidecar folder, embedded in the .blend, or both.

  • Crash recovery

    Bring your history back after a crash, from the last save or the interrupted session.

  • Memory limits & live readout

    Set limits and watch how much memory the history is using, right in the sidebar.

Under the hood

How it stays fast

Ten thousand steps sounds heavy. It is not, because almost nothing is copied.

01

Only what changed

A brush stroke touches a small patch of the mesh. Hindsight stores only the vertices that actually moved, not the whole object.

02

One record, both ways

Each change is saved as the XOR difference of the old and new float bits. The same record turns the old shape into the new one and back again.

03

Compressed

Those difference records are mostly zeros, so they compress very well with zstd, or zlib where zstd is not available.

04

Full copies are rare

A complete copy of the mesh is kept only when the topology changes, such as a remesh. Ordinary strokes never need one.

Scrubbing is a single write

When you drag the slider across many steps, Hindsight does not rebuild the mesh at every one. It folds all the steps of the drag into one buffer and writes the mesh once.

That is why scrubbing feels like moving a slider, not like replaying a recording.

FAQ

Does it replace Blender’s undo?

No. It works alongside Blender’s own undo. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z keep working, and Hindsight adds a much deeper, scrubbable history on top.

How much memory does it use?

Only the vertices that changed in each stroke are stored, and the records are compressed. You can set memory limits in the sidebar and in the add-on preferences, and the sidebar shows a live readout.

Can I share a file with its history?

Yes. Set Save Mode to Embed and save the .blend; the history travels inside the file.

What if Blender crashes?

Use Crash Recovery. By default it restores the Last Saved history, and you can choose Interrupted Session instead to pick up from where Blender stopped.

What about Dyntopo or Multires?

They are not supported in v1. If you use either, a warning appears.

Known limitations

  • No Dyntopo or Multires support.
  • Object transforms are not recorded, so old steps may play back in the wrong place.
  • Modifiers, shape keys, materials, vertex groups and weight paint are not stored.
  • Deform modifiers such as Armature and Displace can give unpredictable results.
  • Animated objects may play back differently.
  • Blender’s Global Undo must be enabled (Preferences > System).
