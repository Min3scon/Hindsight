02What it does

Every stroke kept. Every step within reach.

Blender's undo is made for quick corrections. Hindsight is made for changing your mind an hour later, after a save, a restart, or two hundred strokes.

Rewind & scrubber

Drag back through the whole sculpt

Each stroke, filter or mask change becomes one step, and each object can hold up to 10,000 of them. Drag the scrubber at the bottom of the 3D Viewport and the mesh replays live as you move, or tap Ctrl Z and Ctrl Shift Z to go one stroke at a time.

Going back doesn't delete anything. Later steps wait where they are until you sculpt from an earlier point; then a new branch begins, and if that would throw away a lot of work, Hindsight asks first.

Morph History brush

Paint the past back in, only where you want it

Find a step you liked and choose Set Morph Target Here. Then switch to the Morph History brush and paint over the nose, the ear or the fold you overworked: those areas ease back toward their old shape, and everything you leave alone stays exactly as it is now.

Light strokes nudge, repeated passes get closer, and masked areas are left alone. Each pass is an ordinary step, so it can be undone like any other.

Movie Mode

Turn your history into a timelapse

Switch on Movie Mode in the sidebar and Blender's Timeline takes over the history: press play and the sculpt rebuilds itself from the first stroke. Quick Auto Key sets the keys for you, Frames per Step sets the pace, and you render it like any other animation.

Movie Mode only plays back; it never changes your history. It runs on the global history described below.

Per-object & global history

One timeline per object, or one for the scene

By default every object keeps its own history, so stepping back on the head never touches the hands. Hop between objects with Alt Q and both histories stay intact.

Want the whole session in order? Switch to global history and every object's steps line up on one timeline. With Preserve Modes on, stepping to a change makes its object active again and returns it to the mode it was sculpted in.

Collection Filtering

Keep history only where it's useful

Blacklist the collections you don't want tracked, or whitelist only the ones you do. Objects outside the filter record nothing, and history they already have is hidden rather than deleted: move them back and it returns.

Join & unmerge

Join now, pull apart later

Join objects with Ctrl J and, after a quick confirmation, their histories merge into one. Scrub back past the join and the original objects come back, each with its own history. Scrub forward and they merge again.

Separate (P) asks first and gives the new piece a history of its own. A Shift D duplicate starts fresh, and Alt D warns you that linked duplicates share one mesh.

Persistent history & crash recovery

Close Blender. The history is still there.

History is saved with your work and comes back when you open the file: in a folder beside the .blend, inside the .blend itself, or both.

If Blender crashes, you choose what returns: the history as it was when you last saved, or everything up to the moment the session was interrupted.

  • Embed head.blend + history Inside the .blend. One file to share.

04Features

Everything it does, at a glance

Eighteen pieces that work together. Each one is in the beta you can download today.

  • 10,000-step rewind

    Up to 10,000 steps per object, each one a stroke you can go back to.

  • Viewport scrubber

    A slider along the bottom of the 3D Viewport with a live step counter. Drag it and the mesh follows.

  • History Menu

    Click the dot at the scrubber's left end for history modes, morph tools and the on/off switches.

  • Morph History brush

    Paint chosen areas back toward a marked step while the rest of the mesh stays put. Respects the mask.

  • Movie Mode

    Play the history on the Timeline as a timelapse, then render it like any animation.

  • Per-object history

    Every object keeps its own timeline. Alt Q switches between them without losing either.

  • Global history + Preserve Modes

    One timeline for the whole scene. Preserve Modes returns each object to the mode it was sculpted in.

  • Join & unmerge

    Ctrl J merges histories. Scrub back past the join and the separate objects return.

  • Separate & duplicate aware

    Separate and Shift D give new objects their own history. Alt D warns about shared mesh data.

  • Masks, Face Sets, visibility, colours

    Not just shape: masks, Face Sets, hidden geometry and vertex colours are recorded too.

  • Edit Mode grouping

    Everything you change in Edit Mode becomes one step when you leave. No changes, no step.

  • Voxel Remesh & Trim snapshots

    Topology changes store a full snapshot, so you can scrub straight across a remesh.

  • Branching with a truncation warning

    Sculpt after stepping back and a new branch starts. Past a threshold you set, it asks before dropping future steps.

  • Collection filtering

    Blacklist or whitelist collections to decide which objects are tracked at all.

  • Sidecar, Embed or Both

    Keep history in a folder beside the .blend, inside it, or in both places.

  • Crash recovery

    After a crash, reload the history as last saved, or from the interrupted session.

  • Memory limits & live readout

    Set one limit for all histories; the oldest steps go first. The sidebar shows usage as you work.

  • Works alongside Ctrl+Z

    Ctrl Z steps through Hindsight in Sculpt and Vertex Paint. Everywhere else, Blender's undo is untouched.

05Under the hood

How it stays fast

Ten thousand steps sounds heavy. It isn't, because Hindsight almost never stores a whole mesh.

  1. Only what moved

    A brush stroke touches a small patch of the mesh, so only the points that changed are stored.

  2. One record, both directions

    Each change is kept as the XOR of the old and new float bits. Apply the record once to go forward; apply the same record again to go back.

  3. Packed tight

    Those records are mostly zeros, so older steps compress well (zstd, or zlib where zstd isn't available). The most recent steps stay unpacked for instant scrubbing.

  4. Full copies only for new topology

    A Voxel Remesh or Trim stores a snapshot of the mesh. Ordinary strokes never need one.

  5. One write per drag

    Dragging across many steps folds them into a single buffer, so the mesh is written once, not once per step.

07Beta status

Where the beta stands

Version 1.0.0-beta.1 does everything on this page, and an automated suite checks it inside Blender. What it hasn't had yet is lots of artists using it in the real interface. That's where you come in.

What's tested

  • 125 automated tests, run headless on Blender 4.2 and 5.2.
  • Bit-exact scrubbing: after every jump the mesh matches the recorded step exactly.
  • A 10,000-step stress test.
  • Saving and reloading in all three save modes.
  • Crash recovery.
  • Join and unmerge.
  • Installing the zip with Blender's own extension installer.

Needs real-world testing

  • The on-screen scrubber, and whether dragging it always wins over a brush stroke.
  • Stroke detection: exactly one step per real brush stroke or filter.
  • The viewport refreshing after scrubbing in Sculpt Mode.
  • How Ctrl Z hands over to Blender's own undo.
  • The Morph History tool in the toolbar.
  • The confirmation dialogs.

Known issues

  • If branching drops the step you set as the morph target, the target is cleared. Set a new one after branching.
  • A stroke that exactly reverses the previous one, bit for bit, is treated as an undo rather than a new step.
  • In Sculpt Mode, Movie Mode applies a topology change one tick late, so that exact frame can show the old topology. Render timelapses in Object Mode.
  • A long pause in the middle of a stroke could split it into two steps.

How to help

  1. Try it on a copy of a real project, not the only copy.
  2. Keep saving incremental versions while you test.
  3. When something looks wrong, report it with your Blender version, OS, Hindsight version and the steps that led there.

10FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Something missing? Ask on GitHub.

Is it safe to use on my real projects?

It's a beta, so treat it like one. Hindsight only reads and writes its own history data, and Blender's undo keeps working beside it, but it's new and hasn't been used widely in the real interface yet.

Back up first and keep incremental saves of anything important. The history lives in a folder beside the .blend or inside it, depending on Save Mode. If anything behaves oddly, please report it.

Does it replace Blender's undo?

No. It works alongside it. In Sculpt and Vertex Paint, Ctrl Z steps through Hindsight's history and falls back to Blender's undo when there's nothing left to step. In every other mode Blender's undo is unchanged.

How much memory does it use?

Only the points that changed are stored, and older steps are compressed. You can set a total memory limit in the sidebar or the preferences; when it's reached, the oldest steps go first. The sidebar shows live usage. For measured sizes, see How it stays fast.

Can I share a file together with its history?

Yes. Set Save Mode to Embed in the add-on preferences and the history is stored inside the .blend, so one file carries everything.

What happens if Blender crashes?

That's what Crash Recovery is for. With Last Saved (the default), the file opens with the history as it was at your last save. With Interrupted Session, Hindsight brings back the steps from the session that was cut short.

Does it work with Dyntopo or Multires?

Not yet. When either is active, history pauses and a warning appears in the viewport. Both are on the roadmap.

Which Blender versions does it support?

Blender 4.2 LTS and newer. The automated tests run headless on Blender 4.2 and 5.2.

11Limitations

Known limitations

Worth knowing before you rely on it.

  • No Dyntopo or Multires yetHistory pauses while either is in use.
  • Object transforms aren't recordedMove an object and older steps may play back in the wrong place.
  • Some data isn't storedModifiers, shape keys, materials, vertex groups and weight paint stay out of the history.
  • Deform modifiers can surprise youArmature, Displace and similar modifiers can make playback unpredictable.
  • Animated objectsMay play back differently from how they were recorded.
  • Global Undo must be onCheck Preferences > System in Blender.
