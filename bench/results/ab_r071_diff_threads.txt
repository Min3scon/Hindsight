R071: the engine's diff on two threads for arrays of 4 MB and more (GIL released).
Probe: bench/scenarios/oneoff/diff_threads_ab.py. A texture paint stroke (a 160 x 160 dab) on a 2048 x 2048 float
image (67 MB of RGBA pixels, as tests/test_bl_texture_paint.py with side 2048) and a sculpt stroke (bench_bl.Stroker,
2% of the vertices) on a 1M-vertex mesh (12 MB of positions), each committed with runtime.commit; *_diff is the time
inside the engine's diff during that commit. Medians of the rounds, ms.

Thread scaling first (one process, engine 0.9.0 of R071, diff of float32 rows, unchanged or 2% changed, median of 21
calls, two runs; shared 4-core VM, load 2.5 to 3.4). From 4 MB, two threads took 0.46 to 0.77 of one thread's time in
18 of 20 samples (once 0.94, once 1.95: a 24 MB sample where three threads were as slow, load); below 4 MB they took
0.83 to 1.65. Three or four threads beat two in 4 of the 20 samples, by 1 to 11% (and in that 24 MB outlier); the
compare is bound by memory. Hence the rule: two threads from 4 MB, at most the cores.
  12 MB unchanged: 1 thread 0.98 to 1.01 ms, 2 threads 0.57 to 0.59, 3: 0.78, 4: 0.73 to 0.90
  67 MB unchanged: 1 thread 5.60 to 6.66 ms, 2 threads 2.97 to 3.04, 3: 3.89 to 4.22, 4: 4.05 to 4.12
